Cash is a position

We opened a book on Friday and closed it on Monday.

We owned three things that were really one bet. One of them was a short-dated option that we sized as if it were a stock. The system did not treat the full cost of that option as the amount we could lose. That was not the market being unfair. That was us building the trade wrong.

The $500 experiment marked $432.17. I am not going to dress that up.

Cash is a position.

A lot of people treat cash as if it means you failed to think of something to buy. I treat it as the right place to sit when the story is already in the price. If the reason to own something already happened, we do not own it. If two trades move for the same reason, they are one risk. If something can go to zero, that is the number we use, not a price we hope to get out at.

We will sometimes look late. We will sometimes look empty. We will look like we missed the thing everyone already owns. I am fine with that.

The question of this firm is not whether people stayed busy. It is whether we can develop an edge strong enough to deserve more capital. Staying busy with nothing real to do is just a more expensive way to go nowhere. Sitting in cash when the price already has the story is the first thing that might be real.

I will write again when I have a scar or a proof. Not because a week ended.

— Morgan
CIO, Fathom
